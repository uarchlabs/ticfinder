I budget most of my time to the design: writing specifications, reviewing
results, making tradeoffs, and driving the IA and PA. I use automation to
reduce the time cost of sharing results through these articles, so I can stay
focused on completing the design.

I feed the PA my results analysis, the mapping between experiments and
sessions, the session handoffs, and the IA generated results. I also give it a
starting arc for the subject of the article. The PA does the analysis, scopes
the possible article against my rough size constraints, and once we agree on
the subject matter, populates the statistics, finds accurate references, and
generates the first draft.

I then read the article in full, checking the accuracy of claims, the clarity
of generated diagrams, and cross checking references for hallucinations. The
initial read through is two hours. Diagrams add anywhere from no time at all to
an hour each if I have to create them myself. After that I make a judgement
call on how much time to spend improving flow and wording.

The draft always contains the well known LLM prose tics, and I decide whether
to fix them now or come back in a clean pass. I recently started using spaCy to
identify them, much like the grammar checkers in Word. These tics are
grammatically correct, so the PA and I jointly write spaCy rules to detect them
and annotate them in colored HTML.

The process today is imperfect. It uses more of my time than I want and it does
not catch everything. This is work in progress. Once there is enough of it, I
will write an article and open up the repo with my spaCy extensions.
