## Hypothesis
Some ticfinder constructions became more common in `cs.AR` abstracts after
LLMs became widely used, and others did not. The ones whose rate rose are
measured tics in this register; the flat ones are ordinary academic
English, which is where false positives come from. Candidates for a rise,
from the pacino blog labels and the literature: `EM_DASH`,
`ALTERNATIVE_FRAMING`, `PARTICIPIAL_TAIL` and the `LLM_DICTION` phrase
list. `TRICOLON` is expected to be roughly flat, because coordination is
ordinary in abstracts.

The hypothesis fails if no construction changes beyond its confidence
interval. Report that as a null result: it would mean abstracts are too
compressed a sub-register, or the post-LLM window is too diluted by
human-written papers, to show the effect.

## Background
Kobak et al. measured excess words in post-LLM biomedical abstracts.
Nobody, as far as the roadmap knows, has measured excess
dependency-level constructions. Abstracts are used because they come
with arXiv metadata, which arXiv releases under CC0, and because they are
clean text that needs little masking. Full texts carry mixed licenses and
are LaTeX or PDF; they are out of scope here.

The post-LLM window is a mixture of human-written and LLM-assisted
papers. A rise in rate there understates the LLM rate itself. That is
acceptable for ranking constructions by change.

## Specific Requirements
1. **Fetch script.** Write `eval/arxiv_rates.py` with two subcommands:
   `fetch` and `analyse`.
   - `fetch` pulls `cs.AR` abstracts for two windows by first-version
     submission date: 2018-01-01 to 2021-12-31, and 2025-01-01 to the
     latest available date in 2026. Use primary category `cs.AR` only,
     so the windows are comparable. Record cross-listed papers' counts
     separately but do not include them.
   - Use the arXiv API (`export.arxiv.org/api/query`) or OAI-PMH, with at
     least 3 seconds between requests, as arXiv asks. Send a generic
     User-Agent such as `ticfinder-research/0.1`. Do not put the user's
     name or email in any request.
   - Cache one JSON record per paper (id, first-version date, title,
     abstract, primary category) under `private/arxiv/` (git-ignored).
     A rerun reads the cache and fetches only what is missing.
   - If the API is unreachable or rate-limits, stop and report. Do not
     scrape the website.
2. **Window sizes.** Report how many papers each window has. If either
   window has more than 3,000 papers, sample 3,000 at random with a fixed
   seed recorded in the output, and say so.
3. **Light masking for abstracts.** Abstracts can contain inline LaTeX
   math (`$...$`), `\cite{}`, `\emph{}` and similar. Blank math and
   commands in place (same length, offsets preserved) before analysis,
   keeping the argument text of `\emph{}`-style commands. Do this in
   `arxiv_rates.py`, not in ticfinder. Report the masked share per
   window.
4. **Analyse.** `analyse` runs ticfinder's `analyse()` on each abstract
   (temporary files under `private/arxiv/tmp/`, deleted after) and
   records per paper: words, and finding counts per construction and per
   pattern. Write the per-paper table to `private/arxiv/` (it holds no
   text, but it is large).
5. **Rates and intervals.** For each construction and pattern, per
   window: rate per 1,000 words, with a 95% interval from a bootstrap over
   papers (papers are the unit; findings within a paper are not
   independent). Report the ratio of rates (post over pre) with its
   bootstrap interval. Use at least 1,000 bootstrap draws and record the
   seed.
6. **Sanity checks.**
   - Report the mean abstract length in words per window. A large change
     in length would affect rates.
   - Read 20 random findings from each window for the three
     constructions with the largest change, and say how many are real
     instances of the construction. Do not record the text in any
     committed file; a count is enough.
7. **Commit aggregates only.** Write `eval/arxiv-rates-<date>.md` with:
   the window definitions, paper counts, sample sizes and seeds, the
   ticfinder version, the masked share, the mean length, and the table of
   rates, intervals and ratios per construction and pattern, sorted by
   ratio. No abstract text, titles or paper ids in that file.
8. **Record results** in a new roadmap section 11 (TF-004 results),
   including any null result. Name which constructions rose, which were
   flat, and which fell, and what that implies for the confidence of
   each in the academic register. Fill in the RESULTS block of this
   file.
