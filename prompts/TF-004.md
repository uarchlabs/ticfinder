# Task Overview

Roadmap next step 6: measure how the rate of each ticfinder construction
changed in arXiv `cs.AR` abstracts between 2018 to 2021 and 2025 to 2026.
Both windows are the same venue and subfield, so genre cancels and a
change in rate is mostly LLM influence. This task touches no detector
code and can run alongside TF-003.

Additional task (requirement 9, from TF-003): add `--by-subtype` to
`eval/harvest.py`, so the harvest summary can be grouped by the subtype in
each finding's label text. TF-003 phase 2 needed it and did not build it,
because its step 8 was a null result.

## My Assessment

The pre LLM / post LLM abstract count ratio seems like it would skew the data.
(955 pre, 2,627 post) . IA claims otherwise:

> what (sic) is the number of pre abstract (sic) so much less than the post abstracts  (955 pre, 2,627 post) 2.7:1 ?  

```
The gap comes from how fast cs.AR grew on arXiv. The per-year difference is actually
  bigger than 2.7:1, because the pre window is four years and the post window is under
  two.

  ┌─────────────────────────┬───────────┬────────┬─────────────┐
  │         window          │  length   │ papers │  per year   │
  ├─────────────────────────┼───────────┼────────┼─────────────┤
  │ pre (2018 to 2021)      │ 48 months │ 955    │ about 240   │
  ├─────────────────────────┼───────────┼────────┼─────────────┤
  │ post (2025 to Nov 2026) │ 23 months │ 2,627  │ about 1,370 │
  └─────────────────────────┴───────────┴────────┴─────────────┘

  Per year, that is roughly 5.7 times as many papers. The monthly counts from the fetch
  show steady growth:
  - 2018 to 2019: about 10 a month
  - 2021: about 32 a month
  - 2025: about 93 a month
  - 2026: about 150 a month, with 189 in October

  Likely causes. These are my reading; I did not measure them.
  - Preprint culture in computer architecture. Architecture researchers used to post to
    arXiv much less than ML researchers. ISCA, MICRO and HPCA papers often never
    appeared there. That has shifted towards posting.
  - The ML and LLM hardware boom. Accelerator, memory-system and LLM-serving papers
    make up a large share of the post window, and much of that area didn't exist
    in 2018.
  - General arXiv growth, which has risen year on year across computer science.
  - LLM-assisted writing may raise output. That would be part of the effect being
    measured, not a confound.

  One caveat. The API counts looked low to me for 2018 (250 papers, cross-lists
  included). I couldn't check them against arXiv's listing pages without scraping the
  website, which the task ruled out. If the API index is missing some older papers, the
  pre count is too small. Missing papers would only bias the rates if they differ
  systematically from the ones that were returned.

  Effect on the results
  - The rates are unaffected by the imbalance. Everything is per 1,000 words, and the
    two windows' abstracts are almost the same length (188 and 190 words).
  - The pre estimates are less precise. With 179k words against 500k, the pre side
    mostly sets the width of each ratio's interval. That is why rare constructions land
    in "too few" or have wide intervals.
  - No sampling was needed. Both windows are under the 3,000-paper cap, so every paper
    was analysed.
  - Topic mix is the bigger concern. The growth didn't happen evenly across topics: the
    post window is heavier on ML and LLM hardware. Some of the change in rates could
    come from topic mix and not from LLM writing. Roadmap section 11 notes this, and a
    topic-matched subsample is listed as deferred work.
```

> would it be beneficial to expand this comparison to the paper body rather than limit it to abstracts ?

```
Yes, but as a sampled pilot, not a full rerun. The paper bodies would fix the two
  weakest points of the abstract study, but they bring licensing, conversion and
  run-time costs that abstracts didn't have.

  What the bodies would add
  - Much more text. An abstract is about 190 words; a paper is roughly 5,000 to 10,000.
    Even a few hundred papers per window would hold more words than all 3,582
    abstracts. The pre window's 179k words is what sets most interval widths now, so
    the intervals would narrow considerably.
  - Rare constructions become measurable. NEG_ESCALATION, GAPPED_ANTITHESIS, the cleft
    pattern, CLOSER and HEDGE_STACK all landed in "too few". There is room for them in
    full sentences of discussion that a compressed abstract leaves no space for.
  - EM_DASH becomes testable. The LaTeX source has --- as the author typed it. The task
    would still have to decide whether to count TeX dashes as em dashes.
  - A closer register. The introduction, design and evaluation sections of an
    architecture paper are nearer the design-description register ticfinder is meant
    for than an abstract is. That makes the arXiv ratios a better prior for roadmap
    step 8.
  - Per-section rates. You could test where LLM influence concentrates. Published work
    on LLM use in papers suggests abstracts and introductions carry more of it than
    methods sections. If so, abstracts overstate the paper-wide effect, and comparing
    sections would show it.

  What it costs
  - Licensing. Full texts carry mixed licenses (roadmap 5.3, section 6). Counts only,
    everything cached under private/, and the findings file must stay there, since it
    holds full sentences. The rules exist already; the exposure is larger.
  - Getting the text. Use the LaTeX source (the e-print endpoint at a polite rate, or
    arXiv's S3 bulk buckets), not PDFs. Some papers have no source, which introduces a
    selection effect to report.
  - Masking is the real work. Bodies are full of equations, tables, figure
    environments, algorithm blocks, listings, \cite, macros and \input files. A crude
    conversion leaves fragments that the parser turns into false findings. Doing it
    with only the standard library means a proper LaTeX masker, roughly the size of
    TF-002's code masker, with tests and masked-share checks per file. pandoc would do
    it better but is a new dependency.
  - Run time. The abstracts took 26 minutes, mostly fixed overhead per call. Bodies at
    20 to 40 times the text would take hours for a full run. Downloading 3,582 sources
    at 3 seconds each is about 3 hours more. Sampling keeps both manageable.
  - Weighting. Long papers would dominate pooled rates. Report per-paper rates too, or
    cap words per paper.

  Recommendation. Make it a separate task built as a pilot:
  1. Sample about 200 papers per window that have LaTeX source.
  2. Mask with a stdlib LaTeX masker, starting with test rows and spot-checks of the
     masked share.
  3. Report rates per section (introduction, body, conclusion) next to the abstract
     rates for the same papers.
  4. Go to a full run only if the pilot shows the masking is clean and the rare
     constructions get usable intervals.

  The pilot also tells you whether abstracts were a fair proxy. If the same papers show
  similar ratios in abstracts and bodies, the TF-004 results generalise. If they
  don't, you learn where in a paper LLM constructions concentrate. Either answer feeds
  5.3.

  I can draft it as a TF-005 task file from the template if you want.
```

## Hypothesis
Some ticfinder constructions became more common in `cs.AR` abstracts after
LLMs became widely used, and others did not. The ones whose rate rose are
measured tics in this register; the flat ones are ordinary academic
English, which is where false positives come from. Candidates for a rise,
from the pacino blog labels and the literature: `EM_DASH`,
`ALTERNATIVE_FRAMING`, `PARTICIPIAL_TAIL` and the `LLM_DICTION` phrase
list. `TRICOLON` is expected to be roughly flat, because coordination is
ordinary in abstracts.

The hypothesis fails if no construction changes beyond its confidence
interval. Report that as a null result: it would mean abstracts are too
compressed a sub-register, or the post-LLM window is too diluted by
human-written papers, to show the effect.

## Background
Kobak et al. measured excess words in post-LLM biomedical abstracts.
Nobody, as far as the roadmap knows, has measured excess
dependency-level constructions. Abstracts are used because they come
with arXiv metadata, which arXiv releases under CC0, and because they are
clean text that needs little masking. Full texts carry mixed licenses and
are LaTeX or PDF; they are out of scope here.

The post-LLM window is a mixture of human-written and LLM-assisted
papers. A rise in rate there understates the LLM rate itself. That is
acceptable for ranking constructions by change.
