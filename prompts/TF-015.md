## My Assessment

Phase 1 fixed one source of false alarms, added detection for two forms the detector
  had been missing, and confirmed that nothing else moved.

  Background. The OBJECT_RELATIVE detector looks for phrases where a noun has to wait
  for its verb, such as "every variable the loops assign". "Variable" is what gets
  assigned, but "assign" only arrives after "the loops". It has two versions: the bare
  form with no "that", and the form with "that" ("every variable that the loops
  assign").

  1. Fewer false alarms on the "that" form. In TF-014's reading of your blogs, 3 of the
  5 "that" findings were not this construction at all. They were sentences like "the
  rule that a defect found is fixed", where "that" introduces what the rule says.
  Nothing is missing after "found", so the reader is not waiting for anything. I added
  a check for that shape: the noun comes after a main verb, and another verb follows
  the matched one in the same clause. All 3 of those findings are gone and the 2 real
  ones are kept. The bare form is not affected.

  2. No bad rewrite on "whose" phrases. The detector suggests a rewrite for each
  finding. On "a block whose end the FTB places" it suggested "whose end placed by the
  FTB", which reads badly. Phrases that start with "whose" now get no suggestion. The
  finding itself still appears.

  3. Two forms it used to miss. Both are listed as known gaps in the design note:
  - A verb after a helper verb, as in "a pop the p3 type does not have". The detector
    only checked plain past and present verbs, so "does not have" slipped through. It
    now also handles does, did, can, could, will and would, with or without "not". This
    found 14 new cases in the blogs. In a quick look, 12 were real.
  - "As" clauses missing their object, as in "as the planning documents use". "Use"
    needs an object ("use something"), and here it has none. Verbs where the gap is
    normal are skipped, as in "as you know" or "as the manual describes". So are
    comparisons like "the same names as the documents use". This found 2 cases, both in
    the design note quoting its own example.

  Both new forms report at the lowest confidence level until the corpus runs measure
  them.

  4. Nothing else moved.
  - Every finding from every other detector is identical in the README, the notes and
    all 33 blogs.
  - Every bare-form finding is identical, apart from the one "whose" suggestion that
    was meant to change.
  - No waiver broke.
  - The harvest of your blog edits (which findings you kept, rewrote or deleted) is
    unchanged for every existing pattern. The only new entry is one helper-verb finding
    in a sentence you had deleted.

## Background
Step 15 adds two constructions for a verb that ends its phrase after
the clause's own subject. TF-014 landed them at `low` and measured them

The zero to `that` ratio may be a better signal than either count,
because a register that uses many relative clauses raises both. The
analysis scripts report rates and ratios of rates per construction and
pattern. They have no row for a ratio between two patterns, so this
task adds one.

The `that` form's misses are complement clauses: "that" introduces the
content of a noun such as rule, hypothesis, observation, fact or
claim, and the clause has no gap. In "the rule that a defect found is
fixed", "a defect found" is the subject of "is fixed", and the matched
verb is followed by a finite verb of the same clause.

The design's known gaps (section 5 of the note):
- a verb after an auxiliary: "a pop the p3 type does not have" is not
  flagged, because only finite VBD/VBP/VBZ/VBN verbs are checked;
- an as-parenthetical: "as the planning documents use" is a transitive
  verb in an `as` clause with no object. Verbs that allow the gap
  naturally (know, say, expect, see, note, describe, mention, show,
  explain) must be excluded.
