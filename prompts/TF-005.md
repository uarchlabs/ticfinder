# Task Overview

Roadmap next step 9: a pilot that extends the TF-004 arXiv comparison
from abstracts to paper bodies. About 200 `cs.AR` papers per window,
drawn from the TF-004 set, are measured from their LaTeX source, section
by section, next to the same papers' abstracts. The pilot asks whether
the abstract ratios hold for whole papers and where in a paper the
changes concentrate. It also gives `EM_DASH` a measurement and gives
intervals to constructions that were too rare in abstracts.

Two phases with a stop between them: phase 1 fetches the source and
builds and checks the LaTeX masker; phase 2 runs the analysis.

## Hypothesis
The construction ratios measured on abstracts in TF-004 also hold for
the bodies of the same papers. For each construction and pattern with at
least 20 findings in both windows of both text types, the body ratio
lies inside the abstract ratio's 95% interval.

The alternative, from published work on LLM use in papers: LLM editing
concentrates in abstracts and introductions. Then introduction ratios
match the abstract ratios and body ratios are smaller. Either result is
useful, and the report says which one the data shows.

The hypothesis fails if most constructions with enough findings have a
body ratio outside the abstract interval. The pilot fails as a pilot,
without answering the question, if masking leaves LaTeX residue in more
than 2 of 40 spot-checked sentences per window, or if fewer than 150
papers per window have usable source. Report either outcome as found.

## Background
TF-004 measured 955 and 2,627 primary `cs.AR` abstracts. Abstracts are
about 190 words, so the pre window held 179k words, which set most
interval widths. Several constructions landed in "too few", and
`EM_DASH` found nothing because the API serves ASCII abstracts. A paper
body holds 5,000 to 10,000 words of prose, and its LaTeX source has the
dashes the author typed. The introduction, design and evaluation
sections of an architecture paper are also closer to the design
register ticfinder targets than an abstract is.

The post window still mixes human-written and LLM-assisted papers, and
topic shifted toward ML and LLM hardware (section 11). The pilot does
not remove either effect; it reports the topic caveat as TF-004 did.

## Specific Requirements

### Phase 1: source and masking

1. **Script.** Write `eval/arxiv_bodies.py` with subcommands `fetch`,
   `selftest`, `mask-report` and `analyse`.
2. **Sample.** Shuffle each window's TF-004 primary ids with a fixed,
   recorded seed. Walk the order and keep the first 200 papers per
   window with usable LaTeX source. Record how many were skipped and why
   (no source, PDF only, no main file found, unpack error).
3. **Fetch.** Download source from `https://export.arxiv.org/e-print/<id>`
   with at least 3 seconds between requests and the generic User-Agent
   `ticfinder-research/0.1`. Do not use `arxiv.org` pages. Cache each
   archive under `private/arxiv/source/`; a rerun fetches only what is
   missing. If the endpoint rate-limits or is unreachable, stop and
   report.
4. **Main file.** Unpack in memory or under `private/`. Find the main
   `.tex` file (the one with `\documentclass` and `\begin{document}`),
   expand `\input` and `\include` recursively, and keep the expanded
   text under `private/arxiv/expanded/`. Record papers with more than
   one candidate main file and how the tie was broken.
5. **LaTeX masker.** In `arxiv_bodies.py`, not in ticfinder. Blank in
   place (same length, offsets preserved), never delete:
   - the preamble, comments, and everything after
     `\begin{thebibliography}` or `\bibliography`;
   - math (`$...$`, `\(...\)`, `\[...\]`, and math environments such
     as `equation`, `align`, `gather`, `multline` and their starred
     forms);
   - floats and code (`figure`, `table`, `algorithm`, `algorithmic`,
     `lstlisting`, `verbatim`, `minted`, `tikzpicture`), captions
     included;
   - `\cite`, `\ref`, `\label`, `\url` and their variants, footnotes,
     and command names in general. Text commands such as `\emph{}` keep
     their argument, as in the TF-004 abstract masker;
   - `\item` markers (the item text is kept);
   - the `abstract` environment (abstracts are measured from the API
     text, as in TF-004), the acknowledgements and the appendices.
   - TeX `---` becomes U+2014 followed by two spaces, so offsets hold
     and `EM_DASH` can fire. Record the counts of `---`, spaced ` -- `
     and Unicode em dashes per window.
   Unknown user macros fall under the generic command rule. Report the
   masked share per paper (min, median, max, and how many over 40%).
6. **Sections.** Record section boundaries from `\section` headings
   before they are blanked. Classify each section as `introduction`
   (the first section, or a heading containing "introduction"),
   `conclusion` (headings with "conclusion", "summary" or "future
   work"), `related` (headings with "related work", "background" or
   "prior work") or `body` (everything else). State the rule in the code
   and the report.
7. **Self-test first.** `selftest` runs expected-output rows for the
   masker and the section classifier, written from synthetic LaTeX only
   (no third-party text). Add a row before each masker change, the way
   CLAUDE.md asks for detector rows. Each row checks the masked text and
   that its length equals the input's.
8. **Mask report.** `mask-report` prints the masked shares and the
   skip counts. It also prints 20 random masked sentences per window to
   the console only, for a spot-check of residue (stray braces,
   macro names, math fragments). Count the residue; do not record the
   text anywhere.
9. **Stop.** At the end of phase 1, stop and summarise: files changed,
   the sample and skip counts, masked-share figures, the spot-check
   counts and the self-test result. Suggest a commit message. Do not
   start phase 2 until the user says so.

### Phase 2: analysis

10. **Analyse.** `analyse` runs ticfinder's `analyse()` once per paper
    on the masked expanded text (temporary `.txt` files under
    `private/arxiv/tmp/`, deleted after). Each finding is assigned to
    a section by its offset. Also run the same papers' API abstracts,
    masked with the TF-004 masker. Record per paper and per section:
    words, and finding counts per construction, pattern and subtype
    (the `harvest.py` subtype rule). Write the per-paper table to
    `private/arxiv/`.
11. **Rates.** For each text type (abstract, introduction, body,
    conclusion, related, and all sections together), per window: the
    pooled rate per 1,000 words and the mean of per-paper rates, each
    with a 95% bootstrap interval over papers (at least 1,000 draws,
    recorded seed). Report the post-over-pre ratio with its interval.
    Resample papers once per draw for all text types, so the comparison
    between text types is paired.
12. **Abstract against body.** For each construction and pattern with
    at least 20 findings in both windows of both text types, report
    the ratio of ratios (body over abstract) with its paired interval,
    and whether the body ratio lies inside the abstract interval. The
    same comparison for introduction against body.
13. **Check against TF-004.** The sample's abstract ratios should fall
    inside the TF-004 intervals for the full abstract set. Report any
    that do not; they show what sampling 200 papers costs.
14. **Sanity reading.** As in TF-004: 20 random findings per window for
    the three constructions with the largest body ratio change, among
    rows with at least 20 findings. Also read 20 `EM_DASH` findings per
    window. Count real instances; record no text.
15. **Report.** Write `eval/arxiv-bodies-<date>.md`: sample, seed, skip
    counts, ticfinder version, masked shares, dash counts, words per
    text type, mean paper length, the rate and ratio tables per text
    type, and the abstract-against-body table. Aggregates only.
16. **Record results** in a new roadmap section 12 (TF-005 results) and
    in the step 9 entry of section 7. Say whether the hypothesis held,
    which constructions changed most in bodies, and what that means for
    step 8 (abstract ratios, body ratios, or both as the prior). Say
    whether a full run is worth doing. Fill in the RESULTS block of
    this file.
